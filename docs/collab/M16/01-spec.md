# M16 — Caregiving, adaptation and recovery

User-approved addition, 2026-11-17. Read [delivery rules/dependencies](../M13-20-plan.md). Builds on M2.5 families, M5 costs/housing, M6 time, M14 NPC autonomy and M15 priorities. Deepens existing health and bereavement systems.

## Outcome

Support aging relatives, share care responsibilities, adapt to disability or changed circumstances, grieve and rebuild after setbacks. Care recipients retain agency; meaningful lives and achievements remain available with long-term support needs.

## Playable stages

- **M16a — Care arrangements:** respond to a relative's request, discuss their preference, divide tasks across willing adults, and compare household/time costs with available support.
- **M16b — Adaptation:** adjust work, study, housing and activities to a persistent support need. Play several years with accommodations, assistance and changing responsibilities.
- **M16c — Grief and rebuilding:** experience an established loss or major setback, choose support and new routines, repair relationships and pursue new goals over time. Include a late-life recovery/reinvention journey.

## Content and model

Starter journeys: elder support, a household adapting to a new disability/support need, temporary recovery after a setback, bereavement and rebuilding a routine. Use fictional game costs/outcomes and respectful original writing. Recovery can mean adaptation and renewed participation; it does not require every condition to disappear.

Freeze care agreements, recipient preferences, functional support/accommodation records, tasks/time allocation, payment responsibility and lifecycle events. Share one care arrangement across relatives/unions so one recipient is not charged or processed repeatedly. Adult agreement, child guardianship and ordinary nonromantic family support have explicit separate rules. Existing romantic age/consent protections remain unchanged.

## Browser acceptance and exit checks

1. Arrange support with a relative, accept/refuse specific commitments, share work with another person, and reconcile time and cash across households.
2. Use accommodations to continue a career/project while managing costs; test a $0 starting household with a legal support/decline route.
3. Reload during an agreement or loss event: no duplicated fee, new random prognosis or repeated bereavement reward/penalty.
4. Test recipient refusal, caregiver exhaustion, temporary versus persistent needs, shared households, death and succession. Transition agreements and obligations explicitly.
5. Audit accessibility, privacy and wording. Do not expose private health facts to uninformed NPCs or present fictional calibration as medical advice.

## Traps and ownership

Avoid compulsory care loops, disability as a moral/stat punishment, guaranteed cures from positive choices, or death/grief events repeated for pacing. Do not invent professional support whose cost/availability is never simulated. One owner delivers the full care feature with health/time/finance adapters; D verifies agency, affordable paths and continuity.
